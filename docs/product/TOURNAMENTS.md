# Tournaments

## Decisions
- Four or eight entrants; four expected to be most popular. All six games support cups.
- Single elimination for the championship.
- Losing semifinalists play a third-place match.
- Prize-winning places receive rewards; exact places and allocation require confirmation.
- Complete matches run sequentially; other entrants wait.
- Joining is sufficient consent; there is no extra readiness check before matches. A technical defeat advances the opponent without waiting; silence during automatic play is not absence.
- PvP funds pay entry fees and receive prizes.
- No spectator betting in the first release.

## Format and expected length
**Recommendation:** one game and published best-of format throughout a cup, equal entry fees, random opening pairings, and no purchased bracket advantage.

Four entrants: two semifinals, bronze match, final = four matches.
Eight entrants: four quarterfinals, two semifinals, bronze match, final = eight matches.

At the target 3-5 minutes per match, expect roughly 12-20 minutes for four entrants and 24-40 for eight, before transitions and extra ties. Unlimited tiebreaks mean neither time is guaranteed. Eight-player cups can exceed the original 10-30 minute ambition.

## Player flow
1. Read the game, series rules, entry fee, prize allocation, start condition, and absence policy.
2. Join and commit entry.
3. At lock/start, see the bracket and match order.
4. Watch each complete match and advance after a win.
5. Losing semifinalists contest bronze; finalists contest the championship.
6. Receive prizes and update PvP wealth and tournament history.

**Recommendations:** withdrawal before lock returns entry; a cancelled unfilled cup refunds entries. Voluntary withdrawal or player-caused forfeits after lock do not refund entry. These settlement rules need approval.

## Spectator flow
Follow the bracket, current score, next match, and remaining prize opportunities. React and join playful banter. Quarterfinal losers become spectators; semifinal losers still have a bronze match.

**Recommendation:** bronze before the final, so the champion announcement closes the event. Compact score updates should make waiting understandable.
**Idea:** future nonmonetary predictions; not required at launch.

## Entry fees and prizes
**Owner decision:** the tournament creator chooses the entry fee. **Delegated trial economy:** first receives 50%, second 30%, third 20%. Suggested entry is 100, minimum 10, no maximum beyond available funds. No separate match stakes or bonuses inside a cup. See [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md) for exact examples and rounding.

Prizes should sum to collected fees unless a separately agreed bonus or charge is disclosed. No stake cap applies, but entry must be affordable from the entrant's PvP funds.

At entry 100, a four-player cup pays 200/120/80; an eight-player cup pays 400/240/160. Third place in the four-player cup still loses 20 net. Total prizes exactly equal entries.

## Elimination, ties, and finals
Quarterfinal loss ends play. Semifinal loss ends championship progression but leads to bronze. Final determines first/second; bronze determines third/fourth.

The creator chooses a fixed five/seven-round series or until-victory format according to [GAMES.md](GAMES.md). No final draws and no attempt limit for ties. The meaning of until-victory is documented as a trial interpretation there.
**Recommendation:** bronze and final use the same published series format as earlier rounds.

## Technical defeats and interruptions
**Decision:** joining is enough consent; do not wait for later readiness responses. **Trial rule:** explicit withdrawal after lock is a forfeit. Leaving or not reacting during an automatic series does not establish absence.

**Open questions:** other causes of technical loss, double forfeits, bronze eligibility after withdrawal, and whether an unplayed final awards a title.

These details must be published before entries; no extra grace period is assumed.

**Owner decision:** interruption splits the bank. **Trial interpretation for a cup:** void placements and split the whole unsettled entry pool equally among all original entrants, including eliminated players. Equal fees mean entry is returned to each. No champion or prizes on top. This interpretation needs confirmation if “split” meant a different tournament allocation. A single player's withdrawal is a forfeit, not cup-wide interruption.

## Future ideas
Recurring cups, themed cups, sixteen players, teams, mixed-game cups, and a Grand Final. Round robin is an optional different format, not the chosen initial direction. Asynchronous cups require their own absence/deadline policy and are outside the current no-wait model.

## Test questions
Do waiting entrants stay interested? Is bronze worth playing? Do eight-player cups run too long? Do entry fees exclude friends? Are ties and technical defeats understood without disputes?
